Information Retrieval (IR) is a field concerned with the representation, storage, organization,
and access to information items. The goal of information retrieval systems is to provide users
with accurate and relevant documents in response to their queries.

Indexation plays a fundamental role in information retrieval. It consists of analyzing documents
and extracting meaningful terms that can later be used during search. Modern search engines rely
heavily on inverted indexes to ensure fast query evaluation.

Several retrieval models exist. The Boolean model relies on logical operators such as AND, OR, and
NOT to determine whether a document satisfies a query. While simple, this model does not provide
a ranking mechanism.

The Vector Space Model represents documents and queries as vectors. Each term is assigned a weight
using techniques such as TF, IDF, and TF-IDF. This allows documents to be ranked according to their
relevance.

BM25 is a probabilistic retrieval model that improves upon TF-IDF by considering document length
normalization and term saturation. It is widely used in modern search engines.

Information retrieval systems are used in many domains including search engines, digital libraries,
recommendation systems, and question-answering systems. As data volumes grow, efficient indexing
and retrieval techniques become increasingly important.
